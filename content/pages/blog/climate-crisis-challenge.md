I was asked to join, Joining the Climate Crisis Challenge 2025 was an eye-opening and enriching experience that changed the way I viewed both the climate crisis and my role in tackling it. When I first heard about the competition, I had little knowledge of what climate fatigue truly meant. But as I teamed up with Soma, Yong Qi, and Liang Tze to work on our project, I began to understand how constant exposure to negative climate news could cause people—especially youth—to feel helpless and disengaged.

The challenge asked us to explore how we could raise awareness of climate fatigue and inspire action in a way that felt hopeful rather than overwhelming. After many brainstorming sessions and late-night discussions, we decided to develop the concept of a web app that would address climate fatigue by creating a space that was educational, empowering, and emotionally supportive.

Throughout the process, I took on a variety of responsibilities—from researching the psychology behind climate fatigue to refining our app’s features. Our solution included climate-themed games, curated positive news, interactive quizzes, and a social platform for students to share their sustainability efforts. What set our idea apart was the focus on community, gamification, and real-world impact, all designed to uplift rather than stress users.

One of the proudest moments was when we found out that our team had made it to the top 20 out of 200 teams. While we didn’t progress further, I was incredibly proud of how far we had come. The experience taught me the power of teamwork, the importance of mental well-being in climate action, and the impact of innovative thinking.

Personally, I grew so much through this journey. At the start, I was unsure of what I could contribute, but as we worked together, I found confidence in sharing ideas and presenting our vision. I also learned to listen better, compromise when needed, and stay resilient when things didn’t go according to plan.

Looking back, this experience was more than just a competition—it was a chance to grow as a person, to understand an urgent issue more deeply, and to take action in a meaningful way. It reminded me that even small steps, taken with purpose and hope, can make a difference.
